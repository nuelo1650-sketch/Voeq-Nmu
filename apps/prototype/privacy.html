Privacy Policy

Version 1.0 · Draft for review

1 · Who is responsible

The Media Department of Nigeria Maritime University is the controller of your personal information: it decides why the information is collected and how it is used. Voeq Nigeria Limited operates the platform on our behalf as a processor, and applies our instructions under a written data processing agreement.

If you have a question about the Register, contact the Department. If your question is specifically about how the platform handles data, contact Voeq and we will help route it.

2 · What we collect

About you, when you apply:

  • your name, telephone number, and email address if you give one;
  • your relationship to the business;
  • the device and browser you applied from, and the time of your application;
  • your IP address, retained in a shortened form for security purposes.

About your business, when you apply:

  • the business name, category, description, campus, and location;
  • your promotional material — a flyer, photographs, a menu;
  • links you provide, such as a website or an Instagram page;
  • your written statement of business purpose.

About the decision:

  • the decision taken, its date, and the name of the officer who took it;
  • your response to any request for corrections;
  • payment evidence you upload, including the amount, the date and the bank reference.

We do not ask for, and you should not send us, information such as your national identification number, your bank account details, your health information, or the personal information of your customers.

3 · Why we collect it

We use your personal information for these purposes, and no others:

  • To assess your application. This is the core purpose, and the legal basis is the performance of a task carried out in the public interest and in the exercise of the University's legal powers.
  • To keep the Department's record. The Department maintains a register, so decisions must be recorded and be able to be explained years later. This is the same legal basis.
  • To contact you about your application, your fee, or a change to your entry, and to send your decision and payment instructions.
  • To publish your entry if it is approved, and to display it on voeq.ng. Your agreement to publication is the legal basis for publishing your business information.
  • To protect the Register and our users — preventing fraud, abusive applications, and misuse of the platform. This is our legitimate interest, and your legitimate interests are taken into account.
  • To understand how the Register is used in aggregate, so that we can improve it. Aggregate usage statistics do not identify you.
  • To meet our legal and accounting obligations, including record-keeping and the production of records on lawful request.

Where we rely on legitimate interests, we have considered your interests and concluded that the processing is necessary and proportionate. You can ask us for more detail.

4 · What the public can see

This distinction matters, so we state it plainly.

Published in the public Register, and on voeq.ng:

  • the business name;
  • its category and campus;
  • the description you wrote;
  • your promotional material;
  • any contact details you expressly asked us to publish.

Never published:

  • your name, telephone number and email address;
  • your statement of business purpose, beyond the description you agreed to publish;
  • the declaration you signed and the record of your consent;
  • any correspondence with the Department;
  • payment instructions, receipts, and bank references;
  • your application reference and the age of your entry.

Your application status page is private. It is reachable only with the sign-in link or one-time code issued to you, and it shows your fee breakdown, which is information you need but which is not public.

5 · Who else can see it

  • Media Unit staff of the Department, who need your information to assess your application. The head of the Unit and finance officers see what their role requires.
  • Voeq staff and engineers, on a need-to-know basis, for the purpose of operating and securing the platform. Access is logged.
  • Our processors: cloud hosting, email delivery, private file storage, and payment-evidence storage, each under a written contract and each limited to the instructions we give them.
  • Public authorities, where we are required by law to disclose, or where disclosure is necessary to protect the safety of any person or to investigate fraud.
  • A buyer or successor, if the Register or the platform is transferred, subject to these terms continuing to apply.
  • Nobody else. We do not sell your information, and we do not share it for marketing.

6 · How long we keep it

  • Your application and its decision: retained for as long as the entry is in the Register, and for seven years afterwards, in line with the Department's record-keeping obligations.
  • Published business information: retained until the entry is removed or withdrawn.
  • Payment evidence and bank references: retained for seven years after the entry closes, to support the audit trail and any dispute.
  • Correspondence with you: retained for the same period as the entry it concerns.
  • Unsuccessful applications: retained for two years, so that we can tell you if you apply again, and then deleted.
  • Security logs and technical data: retained for up to 24 months.

7 · Where it is stored

The Register's databases and private file storage are hosted with service providers that hold data outside Nigeria. Access from outside Nigeria may therefore be necessary to administer the platform. Where that happens, the transfer is covered by standard contractual protections, and providers are required to meet the same security standards we apply ourselves.

We keep Nigerian applicant data within the University's control as far as the platform allows, and the Department holds the authoritative decision record.

8 · Automated decisions

Every application is read by a person. We do not use automated scoring, profiling, or machine learning to decide whether an application is approved.

We do use automated matching to help staff find an incoming bank credit against a waiting application — it suggests a match for a human to confirm. It does not confirm a payment on its own, and a payment is only confirmed when an officer records that decision.

9 · Your rights

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act and the NDPA's rules, you have the right to:

  • be informed what we hold about you and how we use it — this policy, and your status page;
  • access a copy of the personal information we hold about you;
  • correct information that is wrong or incomplete;
  • request erasure of information we no longer have a lawful reason to keep;
  • restrict or object to processing where your grounds are valid;
  • request portability of the information you gave us, in a machine-readable format;
  • withdraw consent you have given, where we rely on it;
  • complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

Write to media@nmu.edu.ng. We will respond within 30 days. We may ask you to confirm your identity before we release information, and we will not release the personal information of other people in response to your request.

10 · Security

The Register is served over encrypted connections. Payment receipts and other private documents are stored separately from published listings and are reachable only by authorised staff. Administrative actions are written to an append-only audit log that cannot be edited or deleted, and payment confirmation requires two different officers.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your information, we will tell you and the Registrar without undue delay and will explain what we are doing about it.

11 · International transfers

Some of our service providers process data outside Nigeria. Where your information is transferred, we rely on the standard contractual protections approved by the Nigeria Data Protection Commission, and we have assessed that the transfer is necessary for the service you have asked for. You can ask for a summary of the safeguards we apply.

12 · Children

The Register is for businesses run by students and staff. Most applicants are adults. Where an applicant is under 18, we need the agreement of a parent or guardian before an entry can be published, and we will ask for that agreement during assessment.

13 · Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a way that materially affects you, we will tell you by email and update the date at the top of this page. The version that applied when your application was submitted continues to govern that application.

If you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.
