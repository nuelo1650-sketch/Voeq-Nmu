## Later the same day — decisions taken and prototype built

### Decisions confirmed by the user
1. **Institution = Nigeria Maritime University** (code `NMU`, campuses
   Okerenkoko + Kurutie). The spec's "Niger Delta University of Marine
   Studies" is a conflation with Niger Delta University and was corrected
   throughout. The spec document's title page still needs correcting before
   it is circulated.
2. **Design direction = warm hybrid.** voeq.ng tokens carry the product; a
   maritime-teal NMU layer gives the registry its own identity.
3. **Payment model = not hardcoded.** User wants to discuss their own
   proposal. Both separate and combined arrangements are already supported by
   configuration; the prototype shows the *separate* model (the stricter case).
4. **Build order = design prototype first**, with mock data, before any
   backend work.

### What was built
- `packages/design-system/src/tokens.css` — added the `--nmu-*` brand layer:
  `--nmu-deep #0b3543`, `--nmu-tide #12586b` (6:1 on cream),
  `--nmu-tide-soft #e6eff1`, badge tokens. Cream and deep variants.
- `apps/prototype/` — 7 screens, no build step: landing, six-step apply form,
  applicant status + payment, public directory, Media dashboard, review
  detail, payment verification. All mock data.
  Served with `python -m http.server 8899` from the repo root (the `file:`
  protocol blocks the relative `@import` of the shared token stylesheet, and
  playwright-cli blocks `file:` too).

### Bugs found and fixed during the build
- `tokens.css` header comments used `#` instead of CSS `/* */`. The parser
  treated the comment plus `:root` as one broken selector, so **every token
  was silently dropped** — body rendered serif on white. Always validate a
  token file by checking computed styles, not by eyeballing the file.
- Inline `style="grid-template-columns:..."` **outranks a media query**, so
  the two-column layouts did not collapse on mobile and the side rail
  overlapped the form. Replaced with `.split-side` / `.split-wide` /
  `.split-lead` classes.
- Choice-card title and subtitle were inline spans → ran together. Needed
  `display: block`.
- Form step panes all rendered at once because `render()` was never called
  on init.

### Design decisions worth keeping
- Two environments only: `cream` (applicant portal + directory) and `deep`
  (both dashboards).
- The NMU badge is distinct from the marketplace `Verified` badge — deep
  ground, gold dot, cream text.
- The applicant **status timeline** is the emotional centre of the product.
- **Client-side image compression before upload** (4MB → ~150KB) is the
  highest-value performance decision — students apply on phones with
  expensive data.
- Reviewer's applicant-facing reason and internal notes are **separate
  fields**, with the internal one explicitly labelled "not visible to
  applicant".

## Third pass — the design direction was rebuilt, and the payment pipeline assessed

### The complaint
Kai: *"I hate the design direction totally it feels to basic"* / *"the design
palet feels too basic soo put more intention to the build"* / *"the wordins
everywher about the landing needs more inspirations"* / *"I like the admin but
I dont it doesnt feel effective like the purpose of it feels defeated"* /
*"same for the other forms pages they all feel basic"*.

### Diagnosis (not a palette problem)
The first pass **was** the top AI cliché. The Voeq repo's own creative
exploration (`01-CREATIVE_DIRECTION_EXPLORATION.md` §1.2) names the trap:
*"cream background with a high-contrast serif display and a terracotta accent"*
is the most common generated look. The founder's own `DESIGN_HANDOFF.md` says
it plainly: *"Option-board, not vision"*, *"Generic stock photography … sank
the premium feel"*, *"The palette is fine; the problem is execution, not the
colors."* Adding a fourth hue (maritime teal) made it **more** generic.

Three causes: (1) it was the cliché; (2) cards instead of craft — every piece
of content floated in a white rounded rectangle with a shadow; (3) copy that
said nothing ("Register your business. Properly." fits any university in any
country).

### The new direction — "The Register" (`docs/08-DESIGN-DIRECTION.md`)
A university business register set like a hydrographic chart and a ship's log.
- Palette: paper `#F4EFE2` / ink `#1E3A29` / marine `#12333D` / foil `#9C7A2E`
  / signal `#A8492E` / seal `#2D5A3D`. **The `--nmu-*` teal layer is
  withdrawn** — a fourth hue was the basic move.
- Type: **Fraunces** (display), Inter (UI), **IBM Plex Mono** (every number,
  reference, date, status label). Fraunces has optical sizing, so one family
  sets a 72px masthead and a 20px business name with different proportions.
- Geometry: **radius 0**, **rules not shadows**, no cards. Chips are the one
  deliberate exception.
- Grammar: masthead, folios, rules over boxes, 34–40ch measure, asymmetric
  grid.
- **The chart field** — `apps/prototype/chart.svg`, a generated bathymetric
  contour field (3 seamounts of Catmull-Rom→Bézier rings, 4 shelf paths, 8
  gold mono soundings, 8 plotted business marks with crosshairs) on
  `--marine-deep`. Bold, static, no ambient motion. Used in exactly three
  places: the landing hero, the closing band, the staff queue header.
- The **ship's log** replaces the progress-bar timeline.
- The **register desk**: one dominant queue figure, a ruled work queue, one
  obvious next action per row, a dossier drawer, and the receivable strip.

### The fee split (Kai: *"the split payment should not be public hide it but
dont not say it say it but but publisice it you get"*)

applicant's own authenticated status page, because they must know how much to

### Payment pipeline assessment — `docs/07-PAYMENT-PIPELINE.md`
Kai supplied a school-owned-account architecture (direct student transfers, no
provider, no virtual accounts, **no custody by Voeq**) and asked *"read it and
tell me what you think about these there are different pipeline"*.

Judged **correct** on: no custody, the invariant `revenue = ₦200 × confirmed
registrations`, receivable/agency ledger mode, screenshots never being the
source of truth, maker-checker, the blocking final close, the gate battery.

**The one question that blocks the build** (§2, Q-P1): is the student asked
for ₦5,200 in one transfer, or ₦5,000 with the Department paying the ₦200
separately? The phrase *"the school's manual payment of the ₦200 fee"* admits
both readings, and it changes the copy, the ledger and the receivable model.
A third possibility worth putting to the Department: a flat institutional fee
invoiced to the University, with no per-student platform fee at all — which
removes the whole cash-timing problem.

**Gaps found** (§3): no treatment of orphaned credits and double payments; no
refund path; the "waiting for the bank's daily record" state is unspecified,
which is the applicant-experience risk; two different reconciliations
(daily bank vs institutional remittance) are conflated; no fee-rate
versioning; nine payment tables is too many for 184 applications a year
(reduced to five for phase 1).

**Changes recommended** (§5): chain the audit log **per entity**, not globally;
collapse nine tables to five; version the fee rate; separate the two
reconciliations; make the bank feed pluggable; scope maker-checker to two
actions.

**The business risk underneath** (§4.1): Voeq's revenue is collected from the
Department in arrears with no lever. At 96 registrations the receivable is
₦19,200 — about a quarter of platform revenue sitting with a media
department. Mitigations: a written remittance schedule, a receivable ageing
report on the register desk, and an agreed stop rule.

**Bank evidence feed** (§3.1) — the doc's own open question: use a **daily
statement CSV as the source of truth**, bank alerts only as a same-day
accelerator, read-only access as a later optimisation. Make the feed
pluggable from day one.
