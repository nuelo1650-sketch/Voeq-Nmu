# The Reviewer's Playbook

*Phase 5.7 training material for the NMU Media Unit register desk. Written to
be read in one sitting, then kept open in a tab. The system can force a reason
to exist; only this document can make the reason good.*

---

## 1. What the desk is for

The register exists so the university knows who is genuinely trading on its
campuses, and so a student can trust a badge. Everything below follows from
one principle:

> **The badge is a promise made with the university's name.** Approve what you
> would defend in a staff meeting; reject what you would not.

## 2. The two questions to ask of every application

1. **Is the business real and genuinely theirs?** A real name, a real
   description that matches the photos, an owner who answered their phone.
2. **Is it fit for a university campus?** Nothing in the photos or description
   that the university would not host — no weapons, no gambling, no
   regulated substances, no adult content, no counterfeit branding claimed as
   genuine.

Both yes → approve. Either no → corrections (fixable) or rejection (not).

## 3. Deciding, in order

### The photo gate comes first

The workbench checklist's photo items gate everything else, on purpose. A CAC
certificate you cannot read, an owner photo that is obviously a stock image, a
"shop photo" that is a screenshot — these are not style issues; they are the
difference between a business and a claim. Verify all three before reading
anything else. If an image is unclear, request a better one — **that is a
correction, not a rejection.**

### Approve when

- The three photos are genuine and consistent (same shop in the business
  photo and the owner's context; certificate name matches the owner).
- The description names the actual goods or service, not just the category.
- Campus choice makes sense (a business "on both campuses" needs a reason to
  be plausible — most are on one).
- Nothing in the dossier contradicts anything else.

### Request corrections when — with ONE concrete item per problem

Corrections are the desk's most-used tool and its most-abused. Rules:

- **One item per concrete problem.** "Photo of the certificate is blurred —
  please retake it flat, all four corners visible" — not "photos are bad".
- **Tell them how to fix it, not just that it's wrong.** Every item must be
  actionable by someone who is not looking at your screen.
- **Never ask for anything you would not accept.** Do not request "better"
  photos if the current ones are adequate.
- The optional message is for tone, not for new problems. If it's a problem,
  it's an item.

### Reject when — and only when

- The business cannot be fixed by better evidence: fabricated certificate,
  identity mismatch you cannot resolve, a prohibited trade, an obvious
  impersonation of another business.
- A rejection reason must survive being read aloud to the applicant and to
  the Media Unit head. State the fact, not a feeling: *"The uploaded CAC
  certificate bears a different business name than the application, and the
  ownership could not be verified"*, never *"Suspicious documents"*.
- Rejection is final for that application; the applicant may start a fresh
  one. Do not reject to "ask them to re-do it" — that is what corrections are
  for.

### Suspension / closure (registered businesses only)

- **Suspend** when a registered business's badge has become untrue: the shop
  has closed for the term, the owner has graduated and handed the business to
  someone unverified, credible complaints have arrived. The reason you write
  is shown verbatim on their status page and becomes the badge's public
  answer — write it exactly as you would say it.
- **Lift suspension** when the reason for suspension has been resolved. No
  new reason needed; the suspension's reason already exists.
- **Close** only when the business has permanently ended or the university
  has withdrawn permission. Closure is terminal. Both actions are audited
  with your name attached.

## 4. The fee ledger is a different desk

Review decisions belong to reviewers; **fee confirmation belongs to the
finance role, and the person who staged a match can never confirm it**
(the system enforces this — maker-checker). If you also hold the ledger
role:

- Never confirm because a screenshot looks right. The bank statement CSV is
  the only thing that confirms anything.
- An exception (wrong amount, no reference, odd date) is a **letter to the
  applicant within two working days**, saying exactly what the bank record
  shows and what concern it raises — in the record's own words, never guessed.
- When in doubt, leave it staged and raise it. A staged row waits patiently;
  a wrong confirmation registers a business with the university's name on it.

## 5. Words we use, words we don't

| Write | Not |
| --- | --- |
| "The certificate photo is blurred — retake it flat with all four corners visible." | "Documents unclear." |
| "The description says 'food' — name the dishes you actually sell." | "More detail needed." |
| "The business photo appears to be taken from the internet. Please upload a photo of your actual stall, taken today." | "Photo mismatch detected." |
| "This application has been rejected because the ownership could not be verified: the certificate bears a different name." | "Application unsuccessful." |

No sarcasm, no legalese, no ALL CAPS. The applicant is a student running a
small business with their own money — write to them like a senior colleague.

## 6. The resubmission is not a new application

When an application returns after corrections, the workbench shows it as
**resubmitted — revision N**, with a diff of what changed and the whole
review history. Read the diff first; then look at the flagged items only.
You are not re-reviewing from zero, and the applicant has already fixed
things once — respect the loop by closing it quickly.

## 7. Desk notes

The notes panel is for the next reader: phone calls, promises, context the
form doesn't capture. "Spoke to the owner — new shop photo arrives Friday."
Never anything you would not read aloud in the office; notes are attributed
to your account and never deleted, but they never reach the applicant.

## 8. When you are unsure

Ask the Media Unit head before deciding. An application that waits a day for
a human conversation is fine; a wrong decision in either direction is not.
The reference number in the queue header is how you point someone at the
exact case.
